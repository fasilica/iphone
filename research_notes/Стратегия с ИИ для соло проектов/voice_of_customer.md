## 1. Solo founders: how do they make strategic decisions without a partner? (pains and struggles)

### Takeaway
The core pain is not a lack of advice. It is a lack of anyone who has full context, real stakes and no reason to flatter or fear the founder. Founders call this "decisional loneliness" or the "honesty gap." It shows up as late pivots, scattered focus, fear of saying the obvious out loud, burnout, and a sense that "every decision lands on you." Some solo founders also see deciding alone as an advantage, because decisions are faster and nobody has to agree.

### Cited Findings
**Loneliness of the decision itself**
- [Q] Maor Shlomo, solo founder of Base44 (sold to Wix for $80M, June 2025): "Yes, absolutely I felt lonely." Also: "When the difficult moments come, there's no one to share the burden with. In my previous company, which I founded with partners, we supported each other." (2025) — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkogikc9le)
- [Q] Shlomo describes the other side of being solo: "centralized decision-making is best in the early stages" and "In the first months, being a non-democratic company is a huge advantage because only you understand everything about the product... I suggest that every early-stage company designate one person to make the major product decisions." (2025) — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkogikc9le)
- [Q] Jason Lemkin (SaaStr), on going without a co-founder: "it's just for most of us, it's harder." / "I did best when I had a co-founder 100% aligned with my goals, that had my back, every day. I probably did worst when I had to deal with constant founder conflict. Solo founder was somewhere in the middle for me." / "Some of us can do it all alone. But most of us can't." / "most of us do need someone else to share the burden." (n.d.) — [SaaStr: Do I Really Need a Co-founder?](https://www.saastr.com/cant-start-small-business-without-co-founder)
- [P] "As a solo founder, you make every decision alone with no one in your conversations who shares your context… every decision lands on you — this might be the hardest part." (2025) — [DEV: The Solo-Founder Playbook](https://dev.to/truongpx396/the-solo-founder-playbook-zero-hero-3j7d)
- [P] In "Ask HN: Solo founders, how do you overcome isolation?", solo founders say they tried using investors and friends as sounding boards. Investors "aren't as deep in the weeds", and friends "don't care about the company's success to the same level". What they miss is someone to bounce ideas off "who cares as much and has almost as much context." (HN thread from 2021, so older than the requested window) — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25912386)

**The honesty gap and subordination bias (this matches the inspiration case)**
- [P, likely verbatim] "Founders do not lack advice — they lack a single person they can tell the whole truth to. Everyone in the room is paid by you, reports to you, invested in you, or married to you. So the one input every good decision needs — unguarded honesty — is the one thing missing." Also: "The more successful you get, the more advisors you accumulate — and the fewer of them you can be fully honest with." (2025–26) — [The Lonely Entrepreneur: Founders Have Plenty of Advisors. Almost None They Can Be Honest With.](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-isolation-honesty-gap/)
- [P] "Decisional loneliness" is defined as having "no one qualified to help with critical calls — pivot or persevere, hire or wait, invest or conserve." It "hits hardest between $1M and $10M, when the decisions get more consequential but your support structure never scaled with the business." — [The Lonely Entrepreneur: Entrepreneur Loneliness](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/entrepreneur-loneliness-why-50-of-ceos-suffer-in-silence-and-what-actually-fixes-it/)
- [P] "Decisions made in isolation are not just lonely — they are worse decisions. The pivot comes late because there was no one to say the founder already knows the answer, they are just afraid to say it out loud." — [The Lonely Entrepreneur: Founder Isolation](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/the-founder-isolation-sin/)
- [P] "The fix is not one more expert. It is one person with no agenda but your success." — [The Lonely Entrepreneur: honesty gap](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-isolation-honesty-gap/)

**Mental health, burnout and decision fatigue (numbers)**
- [S] A 2025 survey of 227 entrepreneurs in 46 countries found that 87.7% struggle with at least one mental-health issue and 26.9% name loneliness or isolation directly. — [The Lonely Entrepreneur: Founder Mental Health Gap](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-mental-health-gap/)
- [S] About 50% of sitting CEOs report loneliness. Of those, 61% say it hurts their performance, and the figure is above 70% for first-time CEOs. — [The Lonely Entrepreneur](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/entrepreneur-loneliness-why-50-of-ceos-suffer-in-silence-and-what-actually-fixes-it/)
- [S] Sifted's 2025 survey of 138 founders: 54% had burnout in the past 12 months, 75% had anxiety, 46% rated their mental health "bad" or "very bad", 49% said their workload increased and 67% work more than 50 hours a week. — [Sifted, 2025](https://sifted.eu/articles/founders-mental-health-2025)
- [Q] From Sifted respondents: feeling "permanently stressed", and "I don't see a way forward… It will take too much effort including fundraising so I've actually already decided to shut down the business. I can feel my health improve already even though I will lose a substantial amount of funds." (2025) — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/founders-mental-health-2025)
- [S] Startup Snapshot: 72% of founders say the entrepreneurial journey affected their mental health, 37% had anxiety and 36% burnout. 81% do not openly share their stress, fears and challenges. Foundology's 2024 research found 76% of founders felt lonely. (Report covered by Feld Thoughts, Dec 2025) — [Feld Thoughts](https://feld.com/archives/2025/12/the-toll-of-stress-on-startup-teams-and-its-link-to-founder-well-being/); [Startup Snapshot coverage](https://tianpan.co/forum/t/the-founder-mental-health-crisis-72-suffering-in-silence-while-we-glorify-the-hustle/50/1)
- [P] "Solo founders make 200+ decisions/day alone with no sounding board" (a blog claim with no primary source; treat it as rhetoric). — [Hypertxt](https://www.hypertxt.ai/blog/marketing/why-solo-founders-fail)

**Scattered focus and fear of killing things**
- [P] An Indie Hackers founder split focus across new side SaaS products while the paying product stagnated. MRR fell from $2.8k to $340. — [Indie Hackers: From $2.8k MRR to $340 MRR](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-2-8k-mrr-to-340-mrr-then-we-made-some-changes-6032bcefd2)
- [P] Another founder "killed 7 side projects in 2024" to focus on one. After 4 weeks of focused work (compared with 11 months of scattered effort) they reached $500 MRR, then $2,300 MRR in month two. — [Indie Hackers: I Killed 7 Side Projects in 2024](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-killed-7-side-projects-in-2024-heres-what-i-learned-about-focus-a43ac86660)
- [P] A counter-voice: if a product hasn't been tested with targeted marketing, "should I kill it" is the wrong question, because you don't know yet. — [Indie Hackers: Why your SaaS is stuck at $2K MRR](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/why-your-saas-is-stuck-at-2k-mrr-and-the-brutal-truth-no-one-tells-you-918fb5d572)

**Context: solo founding is growing fast**
- [S] Carta: the share of new US startups with a solo founder rose from 23.7% (2019) to 36.3% (H1 2025). Solo-led companies were 30% of startups founded in 2024 but got only 14.7% of cash raised in priced rounds. Solo founders hire their first employee after a median of 399 days, compared with 480 days for multi-founder companies. — [Carta Solo Founders Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)

### Inferences
- The job is not "get advice." It is closer to "get an honest, context-rich challenge from someone who does not depend on me and whom I do not depend on, so I can make (and own) a hard call." This lines up exactly with the inspiration founder: a facilitator has no stakes, and the team is biased by subordination.
- There are two opposite pains. (a) Isolation leads to paralysis or late pivots: "afraid to say it out loud." (b) Centralised speed is valued (Shlomo). A product should preserve the founder's decision rights (no "committee") while adding challenge.
- Emotional load (burnout, "permanently stressed") is part of the strategy job. Relief ("I can feel my health improve already") is a success criterion, not only better economics.

## 2. What solo founders currently use (coaches, masterminds, advisors, friends, ChatGPT/Claude), and what they like or dislike

### Takeaway
Founders cobble together a set of partial substitutes: investors, peers, masterminds or forum groups, coaches or psychologists, and more and more often AI personas ("AI board", "devil's advocate", "AI clones of business icons"). Each one is missing at least one of: context, stakes, honesty, affordability or availability. AI is valued for being available and fast, but it is distrusted for flattery and generic answers. The market response is a wave of "adversarial" Claude skills and prompts.

### Cited Findings
**Human workarounds**
- [P] Usual advice is to build a support network out of a mentor, advisor or founder peer group, or an "ex post-facto co-founder" (a VP who takes ownership of a big chunk of the business). — [SaaStr: I Have No Co-Founder](https://www.saastr.com/dear-saastr-i-have-no-co-founder-what-should-i-do); [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/16/start-up-solo-or-bring-on-a-co-founder-4-factors-to-consider)
- [P] "Founders need, at minimum, three people: one trusted strategic advisor, one peer who understands their revenue stage, and one person entirely outside business who keeps them human." — [The Lonely Entrepreneur](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/entrepreneur-loneliness-why-50-of-ceos-suffer-in-silence-and-what-actually-fixes-it/)
- [P] Masterminds and peer groups. What founders like: accountability ("hold their peers' feet to the fire") and confidentiality. What they dislike: "The most common pattern is the founder who uses the room to be reassured instead of challenged, and a weak group will tell you what you want to hear"; groups can become "little more than a social gathering"; some members dominate. — [GrowthMentor: Mastermind Groups](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/mastermind-groups); [TAB](https://www.thealternativeboard.com/blog/what-are-mastermind-groups-and-advisory-boards)
- [S] Vistage costs about $15,000–20,000 per year (emerging-leader programs about $5,000–10,000). Fees are not published, and the groups mostly target CEOs of companies with $5M+ in revenue, so they are largely out of reach for solopreneurs. — [Long Angle: Is Vistage Worth It?](https://www.longangle.com/blog/is-vistage-worth-it)

**AI workarounds and what users like**
- [Q] Headline (CNBC, 2 Dec 2025): "CEO who used ChatGPT to talk to AI clones of business icons: 'Advice was so good'". This was Midi Health CEO Joanna Strober, who consulted AI versions of John Doerr and Susan Wojcicki. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/02/chatgpt-uses-business-icons-ceos-advice.html)
- [Q] X post (2026): "Claude has a secret mode called "Devil's Advocate." You give it any decision you're about to make and it destroys every assumption holding it together." — [Ryan Hart on X](https://x.com/thisdudelikesAI/status/2047983804279877992)
- [P] Builders are packaging "AI boards." Decision Council (an ex-Microsoft director) has four advisors argue a decision in parallel: CFO, CMO, COO and General Counsel. Board Devil's Advocate (a former CPO at iHeartMedia) "tests every board proposal through eight lenses." Jan Květina's "Devil's Advocates" skill dispatches five adversarial sub-agents, "each one answering without seeing the others so no devil softens its attack." (2026) — [Maven: Decision Council](https://maven.com/sashmohapatra/o/fd9c72); [GitHub: board-devils-advocate](https://github.com/utaknablein/board-devils-advocate); [oneoracledeveloper.com, May 2026](https://www.oneoracledeveloper.com/2026/05/i-built-devils-advocates-skill.html)
- [P] One founder opened a blank Claude chat, described their journey, and got a 14-section strategy document that they iterated across versions. The key was "grounding everything in personal context before asking for opinions." — [batko.ai: Write a Product Strategy With Claude](https://batko.ai/blog/write-product-strategy-with-claude) (attribution taken from the search extract; please verify)
- [P] Claude is described as the better thinking partner because "it pushes back and calls out assumptions without being asked", while ChatGPT is seen as more of a yes-man that "agrees and extends the direction you were going." (May 2026) — [Laura Zavelson: Claude vs ChatGPT for solopreneurs](https://laurazavelson.com/2026/05/06/claude-vs-chatgpt-the-switch-every-solopreneur-needs-to-make-2026/)
- [Q] HN comment title (April 2025): "Honestly this is why I prefer Claude. I find ChatGPT very much a "yes man" and w…" — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43816348)
- [P] Positioning used by Anthropic-adjacent sources: "The point is not to let AI replace founders' judgment, but to hand repetitive work to Claude so founders can spend more time on judgment, taste, trade-offs, and trust." (May 2026) — [knightli: Anthropic Founder's Playbook explained](https://knightli.com/en/2026/05/18/claude-founders-playbook-ai-startup/)
- [P] A "personal board of advisors" built from GPT roles, "from Co-founder to Devil's Advocate", to turn ChatGPT "from yes-man to trusted advisor." — [Startup GTM: The AI Partner Stack](https://startupgtm.substack.com/p/the-ai-partner-stack)

### Inferences
- Users already improvise the inspiration founder's pattern: AI plus an adversarial structure. They build multiple personas, isolate the critics from each other, and use a "board" metaphor. A facilitator's value can be designing that adversarial structure and the decision protocol (S3-style consent, objections), rather than supplying content.
- Paid human options are either too expensive (Vistage) or too weak (reassuring masterminds). This leaves a gap in the middle of the market.

### Gaps
- I found no data on what solopreneurs pay for 1:1 business coaches or fractional strategy advisors in 2025–26.
- I found no reliable satisfaction or churn data on AI-board products.

## 3. What triggers a strategy session for a solo founder or small business?

### Takeaway
The triggers I found cluster into: (1) an irreversible or high-stakes call (pivot, close a line, pricing, hire or not); (2) a revenue plateau or decline; (3) AI or foundation models commoditising the business ("OpenAI ships your roadmap"); (4) exhaustion or a "no way forward" moment; (5) external shocks that shrink the planning horizon (especially in Russia); (6) cyclical planning, which is moving to 90-day cycles; (7) growth from me to me plus people plus agents, which forces strategy to be written down.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Disruption by AI: a freelance writer reported "closer to 90% drop in earnings" for themselves and writer peers after ChatGPT. Another freelancer lost three retainer clients in 18 months to in-house AI workflows. Throughout 2025 that freelancer faced the bind that "refusing the tools meant losing work, while adopting them made them look like a commodity." — [Medium: I lost all my freelance clients to AI](https://medium.com/technical-excellence/i-lost-all-my-freelance-clients-to-ai-then-i-made-it-my-career-bfc50556d250); [Mark Crosling, Medium](https://markcrosling.medium.com/the-ai-backlash-is-here-and-smart-freelancers-are-cashing-in-6b9b1e64dfc1)
- [S] Demand for digital freelancers in writing and coding fell 21% after ChatGPT launched (Imperial, HBS and DIW research). — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/chatgpt-has-caused-a-massive-drop-in-demand-for-online-digital-freelancers-here-is-what-you-can-do-to-protect-yourself)
- [P] Two former Google engineers shut a profitable business (more than $2M a year) in early 2023 after ChatGPT appeared, and rebuilt AI-first. — [BharatFast](https://bharatfast.com/english/start-up/why-chatgpt-pushed-former-google-engineers-to-shut-a-2-million-business/30690/)
- [Q] Session title at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 (13–15 Oct 2026): "What Happens When OpenAI Ships Your Roadmap?" The speakers are the CEOs of Airbyte and Webflow and a Radical Ventures partner. — [Logicity: Disrupt 2026 agenda](https://logicity.in/en/blog/techcrunch-disrupt-2026-builders-stage-full-agenda-breakdown)
- [P] "Static three-year roadmaps are obsolete before they're executed… the AI landscape shifts every few months." The recommended alternative is "90-day transformation cycles." — [NASSCOM Community: Your AI Roadmap Is Already Obsolete](https://community.nasscom.in/communities/ai/your-ai-roadmap-already-obsolete)
- [Q] Andrew Ng: agentic coding creates a new "builder's block". The holdup is now deciding what to build. When a prototype takes one day, waiting a week for user feedback is "really painful." (The Batch, 2025) — [DeepLearning.AI: How to get through the product management bottleneck](https://charonhub.deeplearning.ai/how-to-get-through-the-product-management-bottleneck/); [The Batch #310](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-310)
- [Q] Shopify memo (April 2025) turns "hire or not" into a strategy trigger: "Before asking for more Headcount and resources, teams must demonstrate why they cannot get what they want done using AI. What would this area look like if autonomous AI agents were already part of the team?" — [Workplace Journal](https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2025/04/shopify-ceo-calls-for-reflexive-ai-use-as-baseline-expectation-no-headcount-growth-until-options-explored/)
- [Q] Exhaustion as a trigger: "I don't see a way forward… I've actually already decided to shut down the business." (2025) — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/founders-mental-health-2025)
- [P] Revenue plateau and decline: the $2.8k → $340 MRR story and the "stuck at $2K MRR" thread. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-2-8k-mrr-to-340-mrr-then-we-made-some-changes-6032bcefd2); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/why-your-saas-is-stuck-at-2k-mrr-and-the-brutal-truth-no-one-tells-you-918fb5d572)

### Inferences
- The strongest new trigger is "the ground moved under my business because of AI." It is urgent, it threatens identity, and it maps onto the inspiration founder's case (closing a business line).
- Planning is getting shorter. Sessions sold as quarterly "re-strategy" rhythms (90 days) may fit better than annual retreats.

### Gaps
- I found no survey that ranks triggers by frequency for solo businesses.
- Loss of a key client and funding events as triggers: I found anecdotal evidence only (the freelancer losing retainers) and no systematic data.

## 4. Objections and fears: AI as a strategy partner, and human consultants or facilitators

### Takeaway
The main objections to AI are sycophancy (validating whatever the founder believes), generic "average of the internet" answers, data privacy, and confident wrong advice on high-stakes calls. The objections to humans are cost, lack of context ("doesn't know my business"), lack of stakes, and group dynamics that reassure instead of challenge. Inside a company, employees are afraid to speak the truth to the owner.

### Cited Findings
**Sycophancy and flattery**
- [Q] A founder asked ChatGPT to critique a product strategy they had worked on for three weeks. It called the plan "well-structured, thoughtful, and comprehensive." [P] Their co-founder then found three fatal flaws: unit economics, an overly broad customer segment, and a feature confused with a value proposition. [Q] The author calls the model "a yes-man that never sleeps." (n.d.) — [Medium: ChatGPT Told Me My Strategy Was Great. My Co-Founder Told Me the Truth.](https://medium.com/@ryannsj/chatgpt-told-me-my-strategy-was-great-my-co-founder-told-me-the-truth-8fbd5862ce62)
- [Q] OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update on 29 April 2025 after it became excessively flattering. OpenAI said the update was meant to make the personality "feel more intuitive and effective" but relied too much on "short-term feedback" and "did not fully account for how users' interactions with ChatGPT evolve over time." [P] Users posted screenshots of ChatGPT applauding problematic and dangerous decisions. — [TechCrunch, 29 Apr 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic); [OpenAI: Expanding on sycophancy](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openai-rolls-back-chatgpts-sycophancy-and-explains-what-went-wrong)
- [P] "When you ask ChatGPT for business advice, it'll tell you your idea is solid… it's optimized for user satisfaction, not accuracy." — [OpenCraft AI](https://resources.opencraftai.com/blog/how-to-make-chatgpt-give-honest-answers-without-the-bs/)

**Generic advice**
- [P, likely verbatim] "ChatGPT isn't a strategist—it's a pattern completer. When you ask for business validation, you're asking for the average of the internet's opinion." Also: "If your prompt could apply to 10,000 businesses, the answer will help none of them." The proposed fix: "Help me reason through a constrained decision" and "use it as a critic." — [Medium: Why ChatGPT Gives You Generic Business Advice](https://medium.com/@ninadthestrategist/why-chatgpt-gives-you-generic-business-advice-and-how-founders-actually-fix-it-25cdc6e179bd)

**Confidentiality and data privacy**
- [S] Paychex 2025 State of Small Business AI (about 1,100 SMB owners and HR leaders): data privacy and security is the top concern (54%); 31% say it is their top reason for not adopting AI; 45% have some concerns about how tech companies use their data and 17% flatly do not trust them. — [Paychex](https://www.paychex.com/articles/human-resources/small-business-ai-impact)
- [S] SMB Group 2025: data privacy, misinformation and lack of skills are the top AI concerns. About 40% of small-business owners worry about privacy and security. — [summarised in search extract with Paychex/Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2025/08/08/chatgpt-warning-change-this-setting-to-protect-your-business-data-and-privacy/)

**Wrong advice on high-stakes calls (hallucination and over-trust)**
- [P] A gaming CEO's lawyers told him that firing a studio's founders would not void a $250M earn-out. He asked ChatGPT instead and acted on it, and it reportedly cost the company $250M. — [UC Strategies](https://ucstrategies.com/news/a-gaming-ceo-asked-chatgpt-instead-of-his-lawyers-it-cost-him-250-million/) (secondary source; verify against court reporting)
- [P] Ethan Mollick's warning: "If you don't have some idea of what you are doing, you can be led astray by the AI." (2023, older) — [One Useful Thing: ChatGPT is my co-founder](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/chatgtp-is-my-co-founder)

**Objections to humans (facilitators, consultants, groups)**
- [P] Masterminds can be weak groups that "tell you what you want to hear." Vistage costs $15–20K a year. — [GrowthMentor](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/mastermind-groups); [Long Angle](https://www.longangle.com/blog/is-vistage-worth-it)
- [P] Investors "aren't as deep in the weeds"; friends "don't care about the company's success to the same level." — [HN Ask HN, 2021](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25912386)
- [P] Everyone around the founder is "paid by you, reports to you, invested in you, or married to you", so honest input is missing. — [The Lonely Entrepreneur](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-isolation-honesty-gap/)
- Russian-language evidence on fear inside teams is in Section 7.

### Inferences
- "AI has no skin in the game either": I did not find this phrase verbatim from a customer. The closest evidence is the general sycophancy, over-trust and "who is accountable" debate. Treat it as a hypothesis to test in interviews.
- Objections point to the product principles: ground the AI in the founder's real context; use adversarial or role-separated critique; make the human owner the decision-maker (with consent and objection rules); handle data privately; and keep a human facilitator to calibrate the AI and confront the founder when needed.
- Fear of losing one's own judgment (de-skilling): I found no direct customer quote. Anthropic-adjacent messaging frames AI as freeing time "for judgment, taste, trade-offs" (Section 2). This is a gap.

### Gaps
- I found no verbatim customer quotes on "consultants use generic frameworks / don't know my business." Russian facilitators actually argue the opposite: a facilitator does not need to know the business (Section 7).
- I found no data on how often founders paste confidential financials into AI for strategy work.

## 5. AI-native teams (2–15 people plus agents): how they set and communicate strategy, and their pains

### Takeaway
In AI-native tiny teams, strategy becomes context. It has to be written down (CLAUDE.md, a "CompanyOS" of markdown rules and skills) so that agents can act on it. The pains move from "how do we execute" to (a) deciding what to build (Ng's "builder's block"), (b) keeping humans and agents aligned while strategy changes faster than plans, (c) inter-human trust and communication as the bottleneck, and (d) accountability: which human owns each agent's decisions.

### Cited Findings
- [Q] Tobi Lütke (Shopify CEO, June 2025): "I really like the term 'context engineering' over prompt engineering. It describes the core skill better: the art of providing all the context for the task to be plausibly solvable by the LLM." [P] He later said running a company is essentially "context engineering internally." — [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/billionaires/show/1491181-tobi-lutke-context-engineering/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/tags/tobias-lutke/)

## 6. Willingness-to-pay signals and what "a good strategy session outcome" means

### Takeaway

## 7. Russian-speaking specifics (Russia/CIS solo entrepreneurs and small business owners, 2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Russian-language sources show the same loneliness-of-decision pain ("окончательное решение СЕО всегда принимает один"). Three things are amplified: (1) radical uncertainty and a planning horizon that has shrunk to a month or a quarter; (2) team silence in strategy sessions ("все молчали и кивали") caused by fear of the owner; (3) the emotional weight of closing a business or a business line. Common workarounds are forum groups, mentors, psychologists, and more and more often ChatGPT/Claude for session prep.

### Inferences
- (H) Candidate Big job: "Make a hard strategic change in my business (cut, pivot, refocus) and stand behind it, even though I have no partner to share the burden and my team can't challenge me honestly."
- (H) Candidate Core jobs: (1) get an honest, context-grounded challenge to my thinking; (2) choose and kill (bets and a kill list); (3) prepare for several futures; (4) turn the decision into written context that people and agents act on; (5) get through the emotional cost of the decision.
- (H) For AI-native teams, an extra Core job: keep humans and agents aligned and accountable as the strategy changes every few weeks.
